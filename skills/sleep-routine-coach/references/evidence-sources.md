# Evidence sources

Checked: **2026-07-29**.

Use only the limited claims summarized here. Recheck the linked primary medical/public-health sources before materially changing health guidance.

## Sleep routine

1. [NHLBI, NIH — Healthy Sleep Habits](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/healthy-sleep-habits)
   Supports keeping broadly consistent bed/wake times, using quiet time before bed, and avoiding bright artificial light and heavy meals near bedtime. Page last updated 2022-03-24.

2. [AASM/Sleep Research Society — Recommended Amount of Sleep for a Healthy Adult](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/adult-sleep-duration-consensus.pdf)
   Consensus statement: adults should generally sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis, while individual needs and circumstances vary. Do not turn this into a diagnosis or a rigid target.

3. [NHLBI, NIH — Sleep/Wake Cycle](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/sleep-wake-cycle)
   Supports explaining circadian timing, light cues, and why timezone/shift changes can affect routines.

4. [Oxford Health NHS — Sleep difficulties/insomnia](https://oxfordhealth.nhs.uk/oxon-adult-adhd/resources/sleep-difficulties-insomnia/)
   Supports gradually moving a delayed bedtime by about 15–30 minutes at a time, keeping regular hours, and reducing evening light exposure. Page reviewed 2025-06-09. The Skill's default two-night hold is a conservative product choice, not a clinical prescription.

5. [NHS Every Mind Matters — How to fall asleep faster and sleep better](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/)
   Supports a set wind-down time, a regular sleep/wake routine, and leaving time to relax before bed.

6. [NHLBI, NIH — Circadian Rhythm Disorders: Types](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/circadian-rhythm-disorders/types)
   Supports the referral boundary: a persistently delayed schedule that interferes with work, school, or social responsibilities may require professional assessment. It does not authorize the Agent to diagnose DSWPD.

7. [NHLBI, NIH — Circadian Rhythm Disorders: Treatment](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/circadian-rhythm-disorders/treatment)
   Supports regular daily/nightly routines and general light-management education. It also documents that light therapy and melatonin are timed treatments with side effects, product variability, and clinical considerations; the Skill therefore does not prescribe either.

8. [AASM — Clinical Practice Guideline for Intrinsic Circadian Rhythm Sleep-Wake Disorders](https://aasm.org/resources/clinicalguidelines/crswd-intrinsic.pdf)
   Supports treating strategically timed melatonin and light interventions as clinical treatment decisions rather than generic reminder advice. Do not derive individual dose or timing from this guideline.

## Nocturia, hydration, and warning signs

9. [NHS — Urinary incontinence](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urinary-incontinence/)
   Supports shifting fluid earlier for people with nocturia while still taking enough fluids during the day. It does not support universal dehydration or a fixed cutoff for everyone.

10. [NIDDK, NIH — Symptoms & Causes of Bladder Control Problems](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/bladder-control-problems/symptoms-causes)
   Supports suggesting professional assessment for waking to use the bathroom and for pain, blood, or other bladder symptoms.

11. [NHLBI, NIH — Sleep Apnea Symptoms](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea/symptoms)
   Lists breathing pauses, loud snoring, gasping, daytime sleepiness, and waking often to urinate among possible sleep-apnea symptoms; recommends discussing symptoms with a healthcare provider. Last updated 2025-01-09.

12. [NIDDK, NIH — Symptoms & Causes of Diabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/symptoms-causes)
   Supports treating increased urination plus unusual thirst as a reason for medical assessment, not as grounds for the Agent to diagnose diabetes.

## Interpretation limits

These sources support general education and referral boundaries. They do not validate individual diagnosis, causal inference from self-tracking, a universal fluid cutoff, use of `goodnight_at` as actual sleep onset, or a guarantee that one phase-shift schedule will work for every person.
